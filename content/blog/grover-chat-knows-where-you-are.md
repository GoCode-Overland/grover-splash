<section id="knows-where-you-are">
<p>You're parked somewhere new, the light's starting to fade, and you need a spot for the night. You open Grover chat and type: "find dispersed camping near me." A few months ago, that message would have needed more from you — a town name, a highway exit, something to anchor the word "near" to an actual place. Otherwise Grover would have to ask.</p>

Not anymore. Grover's chat assistant now has a sense of roughly where you are the moment you start typing. Ask about camping nearby, gas prices down the road, or dog parks close to your current spot, and the assistant already has what it needs to answer. No extra step, no typing in coordinates, no explaining yourself first.

<p>It's a small change that makes a real difference in how trip planning feels in the moment. Less like filling out a form. More like talking to someone who already knows where you're standing.</p>
</section>

<section id="how-it-works">
<h2>Opt In, and Nothing More</h2>

This only works if you've said yes. Grover never puts up a special permission screen just for chat — if you've already opted in to sharing your location with Grover, for the map or for tagging photos, chat can use that same permission. If you haven't opted in, nothing changes: chat answers the way it always has, no popups, no nagging to reconsider.

When location is available, it's used for exactly one thing: helping your assistant understand roughly where you are — enough to know you're near Moab, not exactly which pullout you parked in — so it can actually answer "near me" instead of asking you to spell it out. It's context for that conversation, and nothing else. Not for ads, not for tracking you across the app, not shared with other Grover users.

<div class="feature-highlight">
<p><strong>You're always in control.</strong> Location access for Grover is the same permission you already manage in your phone's settings. Opt in and chat gets sharper about "near me." Leave it off, or turn it off any time, and chat keeps working exactly as it always has — just without that extra context.</p>
</div>

<blockquote>
"We didn't want people to have to explain where they were before Grover could help them. If you've opted in to share your location, the app should already have a sense of where you're standing — nothing more, nothing less."
</blockquote>
</section>

<h2>What You Can Ask Now</h2>

Nothing about how you chat with Grover needs to change. Just ask the way you'd ask a friend who's traveling with you:

<ul>
<li>"Find dispersed camping near me"</li>
<li>"Any dog parks nearby?"</li>
<li>"Best restaurants within 10 miles"</li>
<li>"Current gas prices near me"</li>
</ul>

<p>Chain it with a bigger ask and it holds up just as well. Have your agent line up a spot to camp tonight, a fuel stop for the morning, and a grocery run before the next leg of the drive, all in one conversation. <section id="try-it">
<h2>Nothing to Turn On</h2>

You don't need to flip a setting or hunt through a menu. If Grover already has your permission for the map, chat already has what it needs too. Open the app, tap into a conversation, and ask about what's around you right now.

Dispersed camping, dog parks, gas prices, restaurants — the question doesn't matter as much as the fact that "near me" finally means what you meant it to.

<p>This one's rolling out on iOS first. The Android version of location-aware chat is on its way and will land shortly, so Android vanlifers, hang tight — "near me" is coming for you too.</p>
